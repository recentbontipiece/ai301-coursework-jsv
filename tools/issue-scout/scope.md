## Where candidates come from

Any public repository. The sandbox is behind you; the field is now
all of GitHub, and your fit profile below is what keeps that
searchable. Start from repos in languages and tools you named there,
and let the profile steer where you search, not just how results are
ranked.

**Claims count again.** The Path Review house rule does not travel.
Out here a claim comment or an assignee is a stranger who got there
first, and you have no channel to coordinate with them. Read claim
signals exactly as the rubric's checks state them; do not carry the
classroom habit of claiming over someone.

**Claim upstream the moment you commit.** The rule cuts both ways:
the instant you decide to take an issue, say so on the issue itself
(a short comment: what you plan to do and roughly when). You are now
the claim signal someone else's skill will read. Deciding privately
and claiming later is how two strangers do the same work twice.

## Your fit profile

I am comfortable with Python and JavaScript/TypeScript, and have
experience reading and writing shell scripts. I want to get better at
working with larger Python codebases and understanding real-world
contribution workflows. I would prefer to avoid issues that require
deep domain expertise in unfamiliar areas, and I favor issues with
clear, bounded scope that I can complete confidently as a first
contribution.

Where to search, in priority order:

- (F1) Python projects first: that is the skill I most want to grow,
  so Python repos with maintainer-labeled newcomer issues are my main
  hunting ground.
- (F2) TypeScript/JavaScript projects second: a language I am already
  comfortable in, as a fallback when Python results are thin.
- (F3) Bugs and documentation fixes over new features: a bug with a
  reproduction or a docs gap names its own finish line, which is what
  "clear, bounded scope" means to me.
- (F4) Live, actively maintained projects: I want to learn a real
  contribution workflow, which needs a maintainer who will actually
  review my PR.

Avoid: issues in specialised domains I have no background in
(cryptography, compilers, GPU/CUDA kernels, embedded/firmware, low-level
networking), and issues that are mostly design discussion rather than a
change to make.
